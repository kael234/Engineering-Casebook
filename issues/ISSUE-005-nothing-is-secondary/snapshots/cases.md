## CASE-018 — L'Ambiance Plaza - Lift-Slab Support Loss During Construction
status: draft; revision: 1; first_published_in: ISSUE-005; case_type: deep_dive; evidence_grade: A; source_ids: [SRC-0024].

NBS/NIST identified the most probable initiating event as loss of support at a west-tower lifting jack while an upper three-slab package was being positioned. Laboratory testing reproduced excessive lifting-angle deformation followed by loss of lifting-nut support. The case is used to teach that the temporary lifting arrangement is the active gravity load path during the lift. No unsupported dimensions, jack loads or reinforcement quantities are asserted. [@SRC-0024]

## CASE-019 — Oroville Dam Spillway - Under-Slab Water, Uplift and Erosion
status: draft; revision: 1; first_published_in: ISSUE-005; case_type: site_problem; evidence_grade: A; source_ids: [SRC-0025].

The forensic investigation describes water entry through slab cracks and joints, under-slab uplift where relief was inadequate, and progressive erosion once the foundation was exposed. The transferable lesson is to treat drainage as pressure control and to trace both entry and relief paths. Oroville-specific pressures and foundation parameters are not generalized. [@SRC-0025]

## CASE-020 — Mianus River Bridge - Corrosion-Induced Pin-and-Hanger Displacement
status: draft; revision: 1; first_published_in: ISSUE-005; case_type: detail_product; evidence_grade: A; source_ids: [SRC-0026].

NTSB attributed loss of the suspended span to lateral displacement of a hanger in a pin-and-hanger assembly, with corrosion-induced forces and inspection/maintenance deficiencies central to the failure. The case teaches that critical movement details must be designed for force, movement, water, deterioration and inspectability together. [@SRC-0026]

## CASE-021 — Olive View Medical Center - Instrumented Performance in the Northridge Earthquake
status: draft; revision: 1; first_published_in: ISSUE-005; case_type: engineering_win; evidence_grade: A; source_ids: [SRC-0027].

USGS describes a highly stiff and strong replacement hospital using concrete shear walls in the lower two stories and perimeter steel shear walls above. Recorded Northridge response was approximately 0.91 g free-field, 0.82 g ground floor and 2.31 g roof, with limited structural and nonstructural damage. These are event-specific measurements, not generic design limits. [@SRC-0027]

## CASE-022 — Maule Earthquake - Mechanically Stabilized Earth Wall Performance
status: draft; revision: 1; first_published_in: ISSUE-005; case_type: engineering_win; evidence_grade: A; source_ids: [SRC-0028].

FHWA reconnaissance reported that all mechanically stabilized earth walls observed by the team exceeded expected performance after the 2010 Maule earthquake, while settlement, liquefaction and lateral spreading affected other infrastructure. The case uses this as evidence for robust reinforced-soil system behaviour without claiming that all MSE wall configurations are invulnerable. [@SRC-0028]
