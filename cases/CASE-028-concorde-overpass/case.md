# Executive Summary
A portion of the de la Concorde overpass in Laval, Quebec collapsed on 30 September 2006. The government inquiry investigated the circumstances and causes; later peer-reviewed structural work describes failure of a thick reinforced-concrete cantilever slab after 36 years in service, under essentially self-weight. The published findings point to interacting weaknesses in reinforcement detailing and placement, concrete quality, lack of shear reinforcement, inadequate protection against water and damage associated with repair work. [@SRC-0036] [@SRC-0037]

# Engineering Mechanism
The failed support zone was a thick cantilever slab carrying the ends of the central precast span. This was a discontinuity region where load had to move from the bearing seat into the cantilever and back into the abutment. The structural evidence shows why a member can look massive yet remain vulnerable when the internal shear and anchorage path is brittle, poorly detailed or degraded. Deterioration then acts on an already unforgiving load path rather than creating a completely separate problem. [@SRC-0037]

# Evidence Gap
This record deliberately avoids reproducing bar sizes, spacings, cover, crack dimensions or deterioration quantities that have not been directly extracted from the inquiry material available in this run. The issue figures are mechanism schematics, not as-built reinforcement drawings.

# Engineer's Notebook
At a thick slab, half-joint, corbel or bearing seat, do not let visual mass substitute for a load-path check. Trace bearing reaction, diagonal compression, tie force, anchorage and inspection access through the discontinuity region.
