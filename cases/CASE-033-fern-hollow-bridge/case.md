# Executive Summary
The Fern Hollow Bridge collapsed in Pittsburgh on 28 January 2022. NTSB determined that collapse initiated when a severely corroded transverse tie plate at the southwest leg-to-shoe transition failed. The plate was nonredundant and carried tension generated by the leg geometry. Clogged drainage and debris kept the weathering steel wet, while years of inspection reports documented deterioration without completed repairs. [@SRC-0042] [@SRC-0043]

# Engineering Mechanism
The critical issue was not simply corrosion of a large steel leg. At the taper into the shoe, the transverse tie plate tied the flanges and web together and resisted tension. NTSB corrosion mapping found only about 12.5% of original plate thickness remained over much of the failed plate. The documented drainage condition prevented the wet-dry cycling needed for a protective weathering-steel patina. [@SRC-0042]
