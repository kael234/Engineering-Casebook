# Executive Summary
The 3 August 1997 Ching Cheung Road landslide was the final stage of progressive failure that began on 7 July. GEO's investigation concluded that extremely severe rainfall produced adverse groundwater conditions, including a probably high transient perched water table. Early ground movement disrupted surface drainage and deformed the ground, allowing later rainfall to infiltrate and help drive further collapse. [@SRC-0038]

# Engineering Mechanism
The site had a long history of instability associated with earlier quarrying and old failed ground. The investigation identified subsurface groundwater pathways controlled by natural pipes, partly associated with decomposed basalt dykes in granite and possibly with zones of previous disturbance. Existing raking drains could not prevent critical water pressures. Movement and drainage damage therefore formed a feedback loop: deformation impaired water control, and the resulting infiltration and groundwater response promoted further deformation. [@SRC-0038]

# Evidence Gap
The case does not generalise the site's geology or groundwater geometry to other slopes. The transferable lesson is procedural: once a slope moves, re-check the drainage and groundwater assumptions because the movement may have altered the system being relied upon for stability.

# Engineer's Notebook
After any meaningful slope movement, inspect the drains as structural components. A drain drawn on an old plan is not evidence that water can still reach it, enter it or leave through it.
