# Executive Summary
Skyline Plaza building A-4 collapsed during construction on 2 March 1973. The NBS investigation concluded that premature removal of supporting forms was a contributing factor. Its analysis identified the most likely initiating mode as shear failure around one or more columns in the 23rd-floor flat plate while the slab concrete was still relatively weak. [@SRC-0029]

# Engineering Mechanism
The 23rd-floor slab was not simply carrying its eventual service load. In the NBS Case 1 construction state it also carried the newly poured portion of the 24th-floor slab and formwork. NBS used an estimated average 23rd-floor concrete strength of about 1200 psi for that state, versus the 3000 psi 28-day design strength. Loss of support at one column could overload neighboring supports and allow debris impact to propagate collapse downward. [@SRC-0029]

# Evidence Gap
The exact shoring configuration at the moment of collapse could not be reconstructed with certainty; employee statements conflicted and additional shoring was installed after the event. The magazine therefore presents the NBS analyzed construction state, not an invented as-built shoring map.

# Engineer's Notebook
Concrete capacity belongs to an age, curing history and support state. A strength value on the structural drawings is not automatically available to the construction stage that happens to exist today.
