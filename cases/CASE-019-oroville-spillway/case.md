# Executive Summary
The independent forensic investigation of the 2017 Oroville spillway incident described water entering beneath the chute slab through cracks and joints, producing uplift pressures that could exceed available resistance. Once slab damage exposed variable foundation rock, high-velocity flow drove severe erosion and progressive loss of support. [@SRC-0025]

# Engineering Mechanism
The practical lesson is not confined to dams. A concrete slab on ground can become a hydraulic structure whenever water can enter below it faster than drainage can relieve pressure. Drainage details, joints, crack control, anchorage and foundation preparation act as one system. [@SRC-0025]

# Evidence Gap
The final issue must not transfer Oroville-specific pressures or foundation parameters to ordinary slabs. The case is used to teach the mechanism, not to invent a generic design value.

# Engineer's Notebook
Where water can be trapped beneath a slab, ask two separate questions: how does it get in, and how does it get out? Treat the pressure path as seriously as the gravity load path.
