# Executive Summary
The 22 March 2014 Oso landslide crossed more than a kilometre of the North Fork Stillaguamish River floodplain and killed 43 people. USGS field investigations and testing distinguished slope failure from the mechanism that amplified runout: wet alluvium beneath the moving mass liquefied, reducing basal resistance and allowing comparatively intact landslide blocks to travel long distances. [@SRC-0044] [@SRC-0045]

# Engineering Mechanism
Hundreds of transient sand boils in the runout zone indicated elevated pore-water pressures. Collins and Reid concluded that the underlying wet alluvium liquefied; most of the landslide mass itself did not. The case therefore separates an initiation problem from a consequence problem. [@SRC-0044]

# Evidence Gap
The record does not generalise Oso's runout distance, speed or liquefaction mechanism to other sites. Site-specific stratigraphy, saturation, loading rate and geomorphic evidence remain necessary.

# Engineer's Notebook
A source-slope factor of safety does not define the full hazard footprint. Ask what the failed mass will travel over and whether that ground can lose strength under rapid loading.
