# Executive Summary
On 2 November 2013, five precast concrete beams at the Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport runway expansion fell from their bearings and five more shifted or overturned. OSHA concluded that the beams supporting a roughly 100 ft ductile-iron water pipe initiated the collapse when lateral thrust from the pipe support acted on beams seated on very low-friction sliding bearings that were not restrained against movement. [@SRC-0039]

# Engineering Mechanism
The temporary 12 in water main was supported by steel channels between sloping bottom flanges of adjacent beams. OSHA found that this geometry generated horizontal thrust. The PTFE/stainless-steel bearings were deliberately designed to slide; measured friction was around one percent. Required diaphragms had not yet been cast and bearing restraints were absent. The pipe-support designer had not been given the bridge drawings showing the bearing condition. A seemingly secondary temporary service therefore found a lateral load path through an interface whose intended function was movement. [@SRC-0039]

# Evidence Gap
This record uses OSHA's reported geometry, bearing behavior and causal findings. It does not infer the pipe designer's calculations or assign motives beyond the report's documented coordination failures.

# Engineer's Notebook
Before adding temporary services to an incomplete structure, ask what horizontal force their geometry creates and what actually restrains it today. Permanent diaphragms, ties and fixity that do not yet exist cannot be credited.
