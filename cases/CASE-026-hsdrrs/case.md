# Executive Summary
Following Hurricane Katrina, the Greater New Orleans Hurricane and Storm Damage Risk Reduction System was rebuilt as an integrated perimeter of levees, floodwalls, surge barriers, gates and pump stations. FEMA accredited the system in 2014 as reducing risk from effects associated with the 1-percent annual chance storm. USACE reported nearly 133 miles of defenses strengthened or improved and about 68 miles of interior defenses removed from direct storm-surge exposure by moving the line of defense outward. [@SRC-0033]

# Engineering Mechanism
The win is not a single heroic wall. It is system architecture: barriers reduce the surge entering vulnerable channels, perimeter defenses contain the remaining water, closure structures preserve the line, pump stations manage trapped/interior water and armoring improves resilience when overtopping occurs. The system also makes the maintenance condition explicit: risk reduction is not permanent without future lifts and modifications as settlement, subsidence and sea-level conditions evolve.

# Evidence Gap
FEMA accreditation is a defined risk-reduction determination, not a promise that New Orleans is flood-proof or that every conceivable storm is resisted. Residual risk and future elevation loss remain part of the system.

# Engineer's Notebook
For flood protection, the weakest open gate, low reach, unarmored transition or unavailable pump can matter more than the strongest levee segment. Review the closure of the whole hydraulic boundary.
