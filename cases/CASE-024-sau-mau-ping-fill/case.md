# Executive Summary
The independent review panel formed after the 1976 Sau Mau Ping disaster concluded that water penetration into loose end-tipped fill created a wetted zone, reduced fill strength and led to rapid downhill movement that converted into a destructive mud avalanche. The panel considered inadequate compaction and end-tipping likely to have been widespread in older Hong Kong fill slopes. [@SRC-0030]

# Engineering Mechanism
A fill slope is not defined by its outline. Placement method and density create the soil structure that provides strength. Loose fill can wet quickly during intense rainfall; once a sufficiently deep wetted zone develops, strength can collapse and mobility can increase dramatically. The panel's stabilisation concept included recompacting at least a 3 m surface layer to a 1V:1.5H slope, with drainage, surface protection and safeguards against leaking services. [@SRC-0030]

# Evidence Gap
The review is a system-level post-disaster assessment, not a generic prescription for every fill slope. Modern design still requires site-specific material properties, groundwater/rainfall assessment and stability analysis.

# Engineer's Notebook
Ask how fill was placed, not only what material it is. End-tipped fill and engineered compacted fill may share a soil description while behaving as very different structures.
