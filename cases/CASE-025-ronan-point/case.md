# Executive Summary
A domestic gas explosion at Ronan Point in London on 16 May 1968 blew out load-bearing wall panels at the 18th floor. The official inquiry found that the ensuing partial collapse was inherent in the building's design. NIST's later case history describes a precast wall-and-floor system with no redundancy for load redistribution after local support loss. [@SRC-0031] [@SRC-0032]

# Engineering Mechanism
The local panel was part of a direct vertical bearing stack. Once the exterior bearing wall was lost, upper floors lost support and fell; the impact of falling debris then triggered progressive collapse below. NIST characterises the central issue as lack of structural integrity and alternate load paths. This is the difference between a detail that is strong under its intended force and a system that remains stable after a local component disappears. [@SRC-0032]

# Evidence Gap
The magazine figure is a mechanism schematic, not a reconstruction of exact panel dimensions, bolt layout or gas pressure. NIST notes poor workmanship in some connections but also notes that it had little impact on the initiating event; the case should not be reduced to a workmanship story.

# Engineer's Notebook
For a critical bearing detail, ask the second question: what carries the load if this component is suddenly absent? Robustness starts where ordinary member capacity stops.
