# Executive Summary
During construction of the Penobscot Narrows Bridge, MaineDOT and FHWA tested longer stay cables before and after installation of external viscous dampers. Full-scale forced-vibration measurements established natural frequencies and damping ratios, turning a vibration-control detail into a measured performance question rather than a paper assumption. [@SRC-0040]

# Engineering Mechanism
Before dampers, first-mode damping ratios in the tested stays were about 0.10 to 0.39 percent. After installation they were about 1.22 to 2.21 percent, with FHWA reporting increases by at least a factor of five and sometimes as high as fifteen. In one illustrated cable comparison, a similar acceleration decay took roughly 200 seconds before damping and about 25 seconds after. Frequencies changed little, confirming that the primary intervention was dissipation rather than a meaningful shift in cable stiffness. [@SRC-0040]

# Evidence Gap
The measured results belong to the tested Penobscot stays, excitation levels and damper configuration. They demonstrate a verification method and measured outcome, not a universal damping value for other cable-stayed bridges.

# Engineer's Notebook
Commissioning tests are strongest when they measure the variable the intervention is meant to change. If the design claims added damping, measure damping before and after.
