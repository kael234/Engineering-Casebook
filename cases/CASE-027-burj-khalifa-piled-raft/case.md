# Executive Summary
The Burj Khalifa foundation is a piled raft founded on deep carbonate soils and rocks. The design process combined site investigation, numerical analysis, independent peer review and a substantial pile load-test programme. Trial piles were loaded to as much as 64 MN, and settlement predictions were subsequently compared with measurements taken during construction. [@SRC-0034]

# Engineering Mechanism
A piled raft is not simply a raft that happens to have piles underneath it. The raft, piles and ground participate together, and their interaction governs total and differential settlement. The later peer-reviewed re-assessment found that predicted average and differential settlements from two piled-raft analysis methods were in reasonable agreement with measured data near the end of construction and highlighted the importance of interpreting pile tests with reaction-pile interaction in mind. [@SRC-0035]

# Evidence Gap
The principal design source is project-authored, and the peer-reviewed re-assessment includes authors who were involved in foundation analysis and peer review. This is excellent performance evidence but not independent forensic verification. The magazine therefore avoids turning the case into a universal piled-raft recipe.

# Engineer's Notebook
Use full-scale tests to calibrate the model that actually governs the structure, then keep measuring. A pile test is evidence about a system only after test setup, group interaction and foundation load sharing are understood.
